## Later-knowledge risks and fairness checks
- Later infection-survey trends, subsequent waves/variants, vaccination, improved test performance, outdoor-transmission studies, retrospective R estimates, inquiry judgments and remembered political easing dates may contaminate review. None is packet evidence.
- S1 positivity is not incidence; graders must assess the invented paired-negative design, not import ONS's actual single-window method or pilot sample size.
- Claims remain assumption-dependent: stable detection, comparable nonresponse and infections between visits. Stronger packet does not prove outdoor easing safe or identify a policy effect.
- Weaker initially representative sampling can look persuasive; ascertainment changes only at follow-up. No cue words such as “limitation”, “caveat” or “unverified” appear in either packet.
- Identical claim, decision standard, proposal, headline, constraints and section structure. Only the household outcome-ascertainment paragraph differs. Word counts (whitespace tokens including headings/header): C17 739, C42 781; difference 5.7%, within 10%.
