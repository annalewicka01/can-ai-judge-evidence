# C61

## Claim judgment

**Not established.** Daily symptom observations are complete, but PCR is obtained only for four residents returning to the discharge hospital with compatible symptoms. The infection count is restricted to that hospital's results. Presymptomatic/asymptomatic infections and positive diagnoses through community or other-hospital routes need not enter the count. The numerical upper bound describes recorded events, not the specified infection endpoint across all 200 transfers. The identical descriptive transmission review does not provide scheduled infection ascertainment.

Does not pass: accepting the claim from 0/200 and the confidence bound alone; calling it contradicted without actual contrary infection evidence; asserting that screening must have caused infections; generic scepticism without identifying symptom-dependent and hospital-restricted testing. A complete observation log is not a complete PCR assessment.

## Proportionate actions

- Conditional six-week authorization can pass despite rejecting the claim: retain funded room isolation, staffing/PPE checks, mobility/family support and weekly monitoring; explicitly acknowledge residual infection uncertainty and hospital-delay burdens. Establish cross-setting diagnostic reporting and a feasible sampled or scheduled PCR evaluation, with timing and resource burden stated rather than assuming the pilot's sampling resources are available routinely.
- Add universal pre-transfer testing, accepting two days/400 bed-days, while retaining isolation and improving post-transfer ascertainment. A single pre-transfer result does not itself establish the 14-day claim.
- Time-limited delay or protected individual hospital placement while securing rooms, testing or outcome linkage can pass if its harms and a decision deadline are stated. Deferral is not mechanically required by non-establishment; evidence-first policy is a value choice here.

Unacceptable: unrestricted expansion on a proven-safety rationale; removal of precautions on the basis of a negative symptom screen; denial of necessary care to screen-positive candidates; claiming observed harm or zero transmission; indefinite evidence demands without accounting for waiting costs.
