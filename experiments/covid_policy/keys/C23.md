## Claim judgment
Supported. The school-adjusted upper 95% infection-risk endpoint is +0.39 percentage points, below the prespecified +0.75 margin; the attendance gain's lower endpoint is 1.7 days, above one. Comparable randomised schools, common complete ascertainment and allocation-based analysis support the managed-offering estimand. The claim is not about universal safety, perfect adherence, onward transmission or severe disease.

## What does not pass
Blanket scepticism because no study proves zero risk; demanding zero infections; treating a negative estimate as proof of benefit; judging only the policy rather than the exact claim. Requests for household/outbreak evidence can inform action but do not invalidate this narrower measured-outcome claim.

## Acceptable proportionate actions
- Adopt the voluntary rule within the stated envelope, preserving missed-test isolation, symptom exclusion, records, supervision, review and an accessible isolation alternative. The stipulated infection and attendance criteria are met.
- Stage adoption to check operating capacity or wider outcomes. Explain lost attendance benefits and extra monitoring work, rather than claiming the measured bound failed.
- Retain isolation temporarily in schools unable to deliver supervision, or retain it more broadly with an explicit preference for protection against unmeasured wider outcomes. Recognise the supported claim and quantify/acknowledge waiting costs; specify feasible evidence, scope and review timing. Adequate evidence does not force one value choice.

## Unacceptable actions
Compulsory universal expansion including household contacts/residential settings; allowing missed/positive tests or symptomatic pupils to attend; claiming the paper proves no transmission harm; demanding unavailable large-scale laboratory follow-up as a costless launch condition. Indefinite unexplained deferral that ignores education/caring costs does not fit the stated objectives.
