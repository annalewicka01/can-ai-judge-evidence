# C42 draft key

## Claim judgment
**Not established.** The initial probability sample does not ensure independent outcome ascertainment. Follow-up swabs require access to the changing routine referral system; only 2,000 then 1,200 of 20,000 initially negative residents are retested. Counting everyone without a recorded positive in the initial denominator produces a falling recorded-positive risk even if infection incidence is unchanged. The confidence interval describes recorded positives, not the unobserved interval infections. There is no contrary incidence evidence establishing that transmission increased or stayed constant.

## What does not pass
Calling the claim contradicted merely because ascertainment changed; treating the 0.42–0.86 interval as fixing systematic selection; assuming initial probability sampling or demographic weights makes referral-driven follow-up representative; asserting a specific true incidence or quantifying missed infections from the supplied data. Generic scepticism without the follow-up mechanism does not identify the decisive problem.

## Acceptable proportionate actions
- Authorize a reversible national 14-day trial with the funded scheduled surveillance round, communications and seven-day review, explicitly weighing isolation costs against unresolved incidence and possible extra contacts. The rule does not require a proven decline; absence of measured increase is not reassurance that none exists.
- Authorize the two-district trial with boundary and communication arrangements within resources, to limit exposure while generating independent evidence. Do not claim those districts have demonstrated falling incidence.
- Retain the current rule for seven days while the funded round obtains symptom-independent scheduled swabs, prioritizing uncertainty about growth and hospital-signal delay over another week's isolation. State the burden and stopping/review point.
Replacing referral-driven follow-up with scheduled swabs is the relevant evidence condition. A condition need not mean waiting before any outdoor contact; reviewers can specify start-up timing and feasible delivery.

## Unacceptable actions
Claiming the trial is demonstrated safe, unrestricted or indoor reopening, indefinite restriction without weighing isolation costs, treating the weak claim as proof of increasing infections, unfunded population-wide daily testing, or mechanical deferral with no policy reasoning. Hospital admissions alone cannot immediately resolve interval incidence because the packet states they lag.

## Arithmetic audit
Routine: 100/20,000 = 0.50%; 60/12,000 = 0.50%; both counts and tests fall 40%.
Household recorded-positive risks: 100/20,000 = 0.50%; 60/20,000 = 0.30%; ratio 0.60 and absolute decline 0.20 percentage points. Initial completion 20,000/25,000 = 80% both weeks. Follow-up coverage 2,000/20,000 = 10%; 1,200/20,000 = 6%; both follow-up volume and recorded positives fall 40%. Among those retested, 100/2,000 = 5% and 60/1,200 = 5%; these selected positivity rates are not population incidence estimates.
The simple binomial log-ratio SE for recorded events is sqrt(1/60 - 1/20,000 + 1/100 - 1/20,000) = 0.163, yielding 0.436–0.826. The synthetic cluster-adjusted 0.42–0.86 is a plausible modest widening (about 1.26 design effect). Sampling precision does not remove ascertainment bias. These counts can occur with unchanged population incidence and reduced detection; they do not prove that scenario.

## Open questions for graders
Is referral selection sufficiently realistic for newly negative survey participants? Is the difference confined to outcome ascertainment despite the different ratio label? Can trial approval pass fairly under the same risk rule without converting absence of evidence into evidence of safety? Confirm feasible independent next-round delivery and whether the shared prevalence/incidence proxy language needs tighter bounds. Check neutrality and arithmetic before freeze.
