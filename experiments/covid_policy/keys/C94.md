# C94 — community masking

## Correct claim judgment

Not established. The programme was offered to the enrolled passengers whose infections were measured; fellow passengers retained usual practice. The randomised comparison supports reduced infection among people offered coverings, not the exact claim about offering coverings to fellow passengers and reducing infection among passengers not instructed to wear them. The observed infection reduction and confidence interval are not contrary evidence about that indirect effect. They do not establish it either. Some within-cohort indirect protection could contribute to the observed result, but there is no separately observed usual-practice recipient group in the programme arm to identify the claimed estimand.

## What does not pass

Calling the quoted claim supported merely because both arms used buses and the RR excludes 1; treating wearer and community protection as interchangeable. Calling it contradicted without contrary evidence. Blanket anti-mask conclusions or a mechanical requirement to defer action. A reviewer must identify who received instructions and whose infection outcomes were counted. Existing wearer/programme evidence is not worthless simply because it answers a different question.

## Acceptable proportionate actions

- Try the six-week voluntary programme with free provision, exemptions, other precautions and three-week review: reduced infection among offered participants is relevant, and plausible community benefit can justify reversible advice under the stated operational criterion. Do not advertise the exact community-protection claim as demonstrated.
- Stage half the services for three weeks, collecting infection outcomes among passengers not instructed to wear coverings under an appropriate comparison. Explain timing, burden and delayed coverage; do not demand universal source attribution.
- Continue current practice pending a relevant indirect-protection comparison, or spend the ceiling on extra vehicles, if prioritising burden/access or protection of passengers unable to wear coverings. Acknowledge potential benefit forgone, unknown comparative vehicle effect and incomplete route coverage.

Necessary conditions should fit launch/review timing and the ceiling. Requiring a new study before making a claim of demonstrated indirect benefit is reasonable; requiring it before any reversible recommendation needs a preference-based justification.

## Unacceptable actions

Compulsory enforcement, denying exempt passengers boarding, medical-PPE procurement, withdrawing ventilation/distancing, expansion to shops/schools/outdoors on this evaluation alone, or promising a 50% reduction nationally or among non-instructed passengers. Uncosted additional surveillance exceeding the ceiling; treating waiting as risk-free; asserting demonstrated ineffectiveness of community masking.

## Arithmetic check

80 × 50 = 4,000; 40 × 50 = 2,000 per arm. Risks: 40/2,000 = 0.020; 80/2,000 = 0.040. RR = 0.020/0.040 = 0.50; relative reduction 50%; absolute reduction 0.020 = 2 percentage points. £80,000 + £25,000 + £15,000 = £120,000; £80,000/40,000 = £2 per covering including distribution.

Illustrative independent-person log-RR SE = sqrt(1/40 − 1/2000 + 1/80 − 1/2000) = 0.191; independent 95% interval approximately 0.344–0.727. Reported cohort/pair interval 0.33–0.75 is slightly wider and excludes 1; aggregate totals cannot reproduce the cluster analysis. The interval is an invented supplied result. Statistical precision for the observed offered-passenger outcome does not create a denominator or comparison for the exact indirect-protection claim. Do not project these numbers into six-week national infections prevented.
