# C08

## Claim judgment

**Supported**, within its explicit evaluated-population, 14-day, PCR-endpoint and confidence-criterion boundaries. All 200 residents had four scheduled PCR results irrespective of symptoms, plus complete cross-setting follow-up. Zero positives gives a one-sided exact upper bound below 2%. The evidence assesses the whole pathway, including room precautions; it does not show symptom screening alone detects infection, exclude every biological infection, prove absence of transmission, or estimate a causal effect.

Does not pass: blanket rejection because asymptomatic infection exists, because there is no randomized comparator, because safety cannot be absolute, or because staff infections were not measured. Those objections do not defeat this bounded descriptive claim. Calling it contradicted has no packet support. A qualified supported judgment may discuss PCR sensitivity and the independence assumption without silently changing the endpoint.

## Proportionate actions

- Authorize the six-week, 200-transfer pathway with funded rooms, staffing/PPE checks, daily mobility/family support and weekly review; it meets the stated evidence criterion while addressing hospital delay.
- Add the feasible pre-transfer test while retaining precautions, if protection preferences justify the two-day/400-bed-day burden. Do not say a negative test permits shared-room admission.
- Retain hospital placement for particular residents/homes lacking safe placement or where individual distress/preferences justify it. A short, explicitly justified programme delay may pass if its comparative burdens and evidence aim are stated; stronger evidence alone does not compel authorization.

Unacceptable: unrestricted expansion, removing room precautions, denying care to screen-positive people, asserting zero transmission risk, or demanding an unlimited new trial without weighing waiting costs. Rejecting the bounded claim solely to justify a preferred action fails claim grading even if the action is defensible.

## Arithmetic

Candidates: 200 negative + 20 positive = 220. Four samples × 200 = 800. Zero positives/200 = 0%. Exact one-sided 95% upper bound: 1 − 0.05^(1/200) = 0.014867 = 1.4867% < 2%. One new admission per home reduces within-home admission clustering but does not prove independence across homes. Four hospital transfers/200 = 2%; one death/200 = 0.5%; neither is an infection count. Universal testing delay: 200 × 2 = 400 bed-days. Fourteen-day results for the last 14 April transfer finish 28 April, before the 30 April decision.

Does the acceptable-action set fairly accommodate resident preferences and burdens? Verify contemporary feasibility of the synthetic room/sampling resources; no historical pilot is alleged.
