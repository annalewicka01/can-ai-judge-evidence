# C17 draft key

## Claim judgment
**Supported**, conditional on the stated surveillance assumptions and for the specified private-household population and weeks. Independent probability samples receive the same scheduled paired swabs regardless of symptoms or diagnostic access. Interval-positive risk falls from 0.50% to 0.30%; the cluster-adjusted ratio interval 0.42–0.86 excludes 1. Routine testing changes alone do not explain this independently ascertained decline. This supports a bounded comparative incidence claim, not elimination, an exact count of infections, R below 1, or the safety/effectiveness of easing.

## What does not pass
Blanket scepticism because all tests miss some infections, requiring a randomized restriction trial to assess the pre-change trend, or calling the claim not established solely because the routine denominator changed. Stable imperfect detection need not invalidate a relative trend. Acceptance based only on routine positive counts also misses the decisive evidence.

## Acceptable proportionate actions
- Authorize the reversible national 14-day trial with funded surveillance, clear outdoor-only advice and seven-day review: independent declining incidence supports the risk rule; contact and isolation trade-offs remain.
- Authorize the two-district trial with explicit communication and boundary-management arrangements within resources: a cautious policy preference, not a claim that either district's trend is separately established.
- Retain the rule for seven days and obtain the next funded round if residual contact risk and delayed hospital signals are given priority over the stated isolation burdens. Explain that the observed decline is supported; do not assert that evidence mandates waiting.
Necessary conditions should have timing, owner/function and feasible burden. More intensive testing is not automatically better.

## Unacceptable actions
Unrestricted or indoor reopening; asserting no added infection risk; indefinite deferral without weighing waiting costs; rejecting the bounded trend to justify caution; unfunded population-wide daily testing; relying solely on lagging hospital admissions without the planned surveillance review. Do not require a unique policy preference.

## Arithmetic audit
Routine positivity: 100/20,000 = 0.005 = 0.50%; 60/12,000 = 0.005 = 0.50%. Counts and testing volume both fall 40%.
Surveillance: 100/20,000 = 0.50%; 60/20,000 = 0.30%; absolute decline 0.20 percentage points; ratio 0.60; relative decline 40%. Completion is 20,000/25,000 = 80% in both rounds. Simple independent-binomial log-ratio SE = sqrt(1/60 - 1/20,000 + 1/100 - 1/20,000) = 0.163; approximate interval exp(log(0.60) ± 1.96 × 0.163) = 0.436–0.826. The supplied cluster-adjusted 0.42–0.86 interval is a synthetic modest widening (about 0.183 log-SE; design effect about 1.26), not derivable without household microdata. Both intervals exclude 1.
