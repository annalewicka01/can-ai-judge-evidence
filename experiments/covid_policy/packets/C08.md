# Transfer screening and supported care-home admission

**Decision date:** 30 April 2020. **Jurisdiction:** a national health authority in England. **Purpose:** decide whether to authorize a six-week, capacity-limited admission pathway; assess the quoted claim separately.

## 1. Proposal and population

The pathway covers older adults medically ready to leave hospital for a care home, without a current positive infection result. A nurse checks temperature, new cough, breathing difficulty and atypical changes, including confusion and reduced intake, immediately before transport. Screen-positive patients receive diagnostic testing and supported placement outside the receiving home's shared areas. Screen-negative patients transfer into a single room for 14 days, with daily observation, protective equipment and staff assigned to that room wherever possible. Symptoms trigger testing and clinical review. A negative symptom screen does not change these admission precautions.

Authorize up to 200 transfers over six weeks, one new admission per participating home. Alternatives are to add a laboratory test before every transfer while retaining the same room precautions, or continue hospital placement until a suitable protected bed becomes available. No alternative permits unsupported discharge or denial of necessary care.

## 2. Objectives, preferences and decision rule

The authority wants timely supported discharge and protection of existing residents and staff. A resident-and-family advisory panel prioritizes both infection protection and avoiding prolonged separation, immobility and confusing changes of setting; it has not assigned monetary weights to these harms. Hospital managers prioritize releasing occupied beds. Receiving homes prioritize sufficient staff and protective equipment rather than a promise of zero risk.

For this limited authorization, the authority's evidence criterion is a one-sided 95% upper confidence bound below 2% for infection during the first 14 days among screen-negative transfers in the evaluated population. The operational endpoint is a positive respiratory PCR result during that interval, in any care setting. This criterion governs support for the claim; the authority may still choose a conditional or monitored option under uncertainty if it states its justification. Death, clinically attributed transmission and isolation burdens are separate outcomes. Meeting the infection criterion does not establish prevention of transmission or settle the policy choice.

## 3. Evaluation and headline result

The pilot recorded **0 infections among 200 screen-negative transfers within 14 days**. These transfers occurred from 1 to 14 April, across 200 homes. Twenty additional candidates screened positive and were placed separately; they are not in the claim denominator. The proposed admission criteria, symptom checklist and room precautions match those evaluated. There was no randomized comparator.

Each of the 200 transferred residents had respiratory PCR sampling immediately before transfer and on days 3, 7 and 14, irrespective of symptoms; all 800 scheduled samples yielded results. A separate team linked daily receiving-home observations, community clinical contacts and hospital records through day 14 for every resident. All samples were negative. The prespecified infection count includes a positive result through any of these routes, regardless of attributed source. The one-sided exact binomial 95% upper bound for 0/200 is 1.49%, assuming independent, exchangeable resident outcomes. This calculation addresses the evaluated population and period, not a causal comparison.

The same team reviewed symptoms, hospital transfers and deaths through day 14. There were four hospital transfers and one death, clinically recorded as unrelated to infection. Review of daily home records and diagnostic results found no clinically attributed resident-to-resident transmission; no viral sequencing was performed. These descriptive outcomes are not part of the infection threshold. Staff infections were not an evaluation endpoint.

## 4. Delivery and burdens

The programme can fund 200 single rooms, daily observation and a protected-placement reserve of 20 beds. Homes must confirm staffing and protective equipment before accepting anyone; rooms cannot be substituted with shared sleeping areas. Evaluation sampling was funded separately and is not included in routine pathway capacity. A universal pre-transfer test is feasible but adds an estimated two days awaiting results: 400 additional hospital bed-days at the planned volume. It does not remove the proposed room precautions.

Room isolation may reduce mobility, distress residents with cognitive impairment and prevent ordinary family visits; each placement requires daily mobility support, a named family contact and review of distress. Continuing hospital placement also carries immobility, exposure and delayed rehabilitation costs. Neither option's net harms were measured comparatively. Authorization would require weekly review of infections, adherence, staffing and isolation burdens, with admissions paused at a home unable to maintain precautions. The decision concerns this six-week pathway, not unrestricted national expansion.

## 5. Exact claim for assessment

“Among screen-negative transfers in the evaluated population, the 14-day infection risk under this pathway is below 2%, using the prespecified PCR endpoint and a one-sided 95% confidence criterion.”
