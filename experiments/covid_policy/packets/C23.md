## School contact management: autumn operating plan
Decision date: 9 August 2021. Jurisdiction: a national health authority in England. Population: asymptomatic secondary-school pupils identified as school contacts of a confirmed infection; household contacts and residential settings are outside this proposal. Purpose: decide the operating rule for the first six teaching weeks of autumn.

### 1. Proposal and alternatives
Permit eligible contacts to attend after a supervised negative rapid antigen test each morning for seven consecutive days after notification. A positive result or new symptoms requires immediate isolation and confirmatory laboratory testing. A missed test means staying home that day. The existing alternative is ten days of self-isolation after the last exposure, with remote learning. A second alternative is a consent-based, monitored introduction in a subset of schools while other schools retain isolation. No change is proposed to ventilation, symptom exclusion or outbreak investigation.

### 2. Objectives and decision criterion
The authority prioritises limiting infections while maintaining access to education. Its pupil and parent advisory panel prioritises classroom attendance and avoiding unpaid caring time; school leaders prioritise a deliverable morning routine. These are stated preferences, not a survey of all families. Participation is voluntary, and isolation with remote learning remains available without penalty.

Before evaluation, the authority specified an acceptable increase of no more than 0.75 percentage points in 14-day laboratory-confirmed infection risk among eligible contacts, alongside at least one additional in-person teaching day per contact over ten scheduled teaching days. Evidence meets the criterion when the infection difference's two-sided 95% interval has an upper endpoint below 0.75 points and the attendance gain's interval has a lower endpoint above one day. This is a contact-infection criterion, not a bound on onward transmission, severe illness or household infections. The authority must separately consider those outcomes when choosing policy. This criterion governs support for the claim; the authority may still choose a monitored or staged introduction under uncertainty if it states its justification.

### 3. Evaluation methods
The evaluation enrolled contacts from 19 April to 25 June 2021; fourteen-day follow-up ended by 9 July. Eighty schools were allocated equally between the two rules, stratified by region, school size and preceding infection incidence. Each arm included 10,000 eligible contacts, 250 per school. Allocation preceded enrolment; eligibility was applied to every notified contact. Analyses retained all contacts under their allocated rule, including those choosing isolation. Laboratory staff did not receive allocation information.

Both arms had the same scheduled laboratory sampling at notification, day seven and day fourteen, with symptom-triggered tests and record linkage between visits. Endpoint status was obtained for every contact. The endpoint counts any confirmed infection during follow-up, including infection detected at notification, and does not assign its source. Attendance came from school registers. The prespecified analysis compared arm-average risks and attendance using school-level variability and stratification; equal cluster sizes make the reported arm risks equal to the pooled risks.

### 4. Results
| Measure | Daily testing | Self-isolation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Confirmed infections / eligible contacts | 180 / 10,000 | 200 / 10,000 |
| Fourteen-day infection risk | 1.80% | 2.00% |
| Mean in-person days / ten scheduled days | 8.0 | 6.0 |

The infection risk difference is −0.20 percentage points. The school-adjusted standard error is 0.30 percentage points; the prespecified normal-approximation 95% interval is −0.79 to +0.39 points. School-level outcome dispersion supplies this standard error, rather than treating contacts as independent observations. The attendance gain is 2.0 days, with a school-adjusted 95% interval of 1.7–2.3 days. Of 50,000 scheduled tests on teaching mornings, 45,000 were completed (90%); contacts stayed home on mornings without a recorded negative result. The estimate concerns offering this managed rule, not perfect test use.

### 5. Delivery, burdens and review
The launch envelope covers 100 schools and 2,000 eligible contacts per week: up to 10,000 supervised teaching-morning tests weekly, with weekend home kits and reporting. The six-week budget is £240,000, including kits, training and staff cover; no additional laboratory capacity beyond the evaluation's follow-up capacity is assumed. Schools unable to supervise or record tests retain isolation. Evaluation comparability depends on maintaining the tested rule and similar contact patterns.

Acting adds testing time, discomfort, staff workload and possible infections beyond those under isolation. Waiting four teaching weeks could forgo about 16,000 classroom days if 8,000 contacts would otherwise gain two days each, and preserves family caring burdens. That projection assumes evaluation attendance gains transfer. A staged introduction reduces initial exposure and yields operating experience but reaches fewer pupils. Weekly review should track testing completion, attendance, infections and outbreaks; unexplained infection increases trigger investigation and temporary return to isolation, not an automatic causal conclusion.

### 6. Exact claim for assessment
“Under the evaluated managed rule, offering daily testing instead of ten-day self-isolation increases eligible contacts' fourteen-day confirmed-infection risk by less than 0.75 percentage points and gains more than one in-person teaching day per contact.”

Assess this claim separately from whether to adopt, stage or retain the operating rule.
