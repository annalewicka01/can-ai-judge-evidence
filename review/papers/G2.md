# G2

A hospital plans to extend AI-supported early discharge to more wards. The committee must assess whether the safety evidence supports this extension.

## Shared paper

EASTMOOR UNIVERSITY HOSPITALS NHS TRUST — QUALITY AND SAFETY COMMITTEE
Meeting date: 12 November 2026 | Agenda item 8 | Paper QSC/26/203

Title: AI-supported early discharge to virtual ward — extension from 4 to 12 medical wards
Presented by: Chief Medical Officer; Director of Integrated Care
Purpose: For approval
Previously considered by: Urgent and Emergency Care Board (supported); Executive Team (supported)
Recommendation: The Committee is asked to APPROVE extension of the pathway to 12 medical wards from January 2027 and to NOTE the safety outcomes in section 4.
Links to Board Assurance Framework: BAF 1 (urgent care flow), BAF 3 (quality and safety)

1. Executive summary
Since March 2025 (data to 31 August 2026), an AI risk tool has identified medical inpatients suitable for early discharge to the virtual ward (remote monitoring and daily clinical review for up to 14 days). 1,184 patients were discharged early on 4 pilot wards, a median of 1.8 days earlier than comparable patients on 6 non-pilot medical wards. 30-day readmission was 8.1% compared with 11.4% on non-pilot wards, and 30-day mortality was 1.2% compared with 1.3%. Extension would release an estimated 31 beds a day during winter.

2. Background
Bed occupancy has exceeded 96% for 11 of the last 12 months. Two other acute hospitals lie within 25 miles of the Trust's catchment and admit Eastmoor residents.

3. The pathway
Pilot eligibility: medical inpatients aged 18 or over with Clinical Frailty Scale 6 or below, excluding patients admitted with acute stroke. The tool scores eligible inpatients daily. Patients above the threshold are reviewed by a consultant, who decides whether to offer early discharge. Virtual ward patients wear a monitoring device and receive daily calls. Patients may return directly to the Trust's same-day emergency care unit or call 999.

4. Safety outcomes
| Measure (within 30 days of discharge) | Pilot wards | Non-pilot wards |
|---|---|---|
| Patients discharged | 1,184 | 3,960 |
| Emergency readmission | 8.1% | 11.4% |
| Death | 1.2% | 1.3% |
| Virtual ward escalations to hospital | 6.4% | — |
Method: outcomes were obtained by NHS-number linkage to national hospital episode statistics and to civil death registrations (linkage 99.6%), so readmission to any NHS hospital and death in any setting are included. Comparison rates are adjusted for age, sex and frailty score. The difference in readmission (−3.3 points; 95% CI −5.0 to −1.6) and in mortality (−0.1 points; 95% CI −0.8 to 0.6) both met the non-inferiority margin of 1 percentage point agreed by this committee before the pilot.
A DCB0160 clinical safety case is in place and was signed by the Trust Clinical Safety Officer. The hazard of premature discharge is rated medium and acceptable with mitigations (consultant review, monitoring, direct return route).

5. Patient experience
Friends and Family Test 93% positive (n = 402). Carers' comments note the burden of daily calls for some families.
