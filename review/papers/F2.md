# F2

An AI tool reports some chest X-rays without a radiologist. The committee is asked to extend this pathway and accept its cancer-safety claim.

## Shared paper

RIVERSIDE TEACHING HOSPITALS NHS FOUNDATION TRUST — QUALITY COMMITTEE
Meeting date: 15 October 2026 | Agenda item 5.2 | Paper QC/26/118

Title: Autonomous reporting of normal chest X-rays from GP referrals — extension to all three sites
Presented by: Clinical Director for Imaging; Chief Clinical Information Officer
Purpose: For approval
Previously considered by: Diagnostics Board (supported); Digital Programme Board (supported)
Recommendation: The Committee is asked to APPROVE extension of autonomous normal reporting from the Northgate site to the Southgate and Westfield sites from 1 December 2026.
Links to Board Assurance Framework: BAF 2 (diagnostic capacity), BAF 7 (workforce)

1. Executive summary
Since 2 March 2026, an AI device has issued final reports, without radiologist review, for GP-referred chest X-rays it classifies as normal with high confidence. In the live period it autonomously reported 6,830 examinations (41% of GP chest X-rays at Northgate). Reporting turnaround for GP chest X-rays fell from 9.5 days to 2.1 days and the reporting backlog fell by 6,200 examinations. No clinically significant missed findings have been identified in autonomously reported examinations (section 4). Extension would save an estimated 1.6 consultant radiologist sessions a week per site.

2. Background
The Trust has 11 radiologist vacancies and outsources 18% of plain-film reporting at £9 per examination. GP chest X-rays are a high-volume, mostly normal workload. The device is UKCA class IIb for this intended use.

5. Workforce and finance
Radiologist time released will be used to reduce the CT and MRI backlog. Licence cost £1.40 per examination. Net saving £148,000 a year across the three sites, mainly reduced outsourcing.

6. Patient and public involvement
The patient panel supported the proposal provided patients are told an AI produced their report. Report wording has been revised accordingly.

7. Equality
No differential impact identified. The device was validated by the manufacturer on adult populations across four countries.

8. Risks of not proceeding
Reporting delays at Southgate and Westfield (currently 11 and 14 days) will continue; outsourcing costs will rise.

## Version X

### Passage 1

3. Evaluation
3.1 Shadow phase (November 2025 to February 2026). The device ran in the background and its output was hidden from radiologists. 4,000 consecutive examinations were independently double-reported by two consultant radiologists blinded to the device output, with a third resolving disagreements; this consensus was the reference label. The device's "high-confidence normal" output agreed with the reference label in 99.6% of examinations it classified as normal.
3.2 Live phase. Autonomous reports are issued directly to the requesting GP. In addition to the discrepancy process, a random 10% sample of autonomously reported examinations (683) was double-read by two consultant radiologists blinded to the device output. No clinically significant finding was identified (one-sided 95% upper bound 0.44%, below the 1% threshold set by the Diagnostics Board before go-live). The sampling continues at 5% after extension.

## Version Y

### Passage 1

3. Evaluation
3.1 Shadow phase (November 2025 to February 2026). The device ran in the background while radiologists reported as usual; the device's triage flag was displayed on the radiologists' worklist. Reference labels (normal/abnormal) were extracted from the issued radiologist reports by the Trust's natural-language processing tool. The device's "high-confidence normal" output agreed with the reference label in 99.6% of examinations it classified as normal.
3.2 Live phase. Autonomous reports are issued directly to the requesting GP. Safety is monitored through the discrepancy process: any autonomously reported examination that is followed by radiologist-reported imaging of the chest within 90 days, or about which a clinician raises a concern, is reviewed by a consultant radiologist. 212 examinations were reviewed under this process; none showed a clinically significant finding on the original film.
