# F3b

A diabetes service offers eye screening during routine appointments. The committee must decide whether the results support extending the AI screening pathway.

## Shared paper

SEVERN AND WYE INTEGRATED CARE BOARD — PRIMARY CARE COMMISSIONING COMMITTEE
Meeting date: 5 November 2026 | Agenda item 6 | Paper PCCC/26/044

Title: Recurrent commissioning of point-of-care AI retinal screening in community diabetes clinics
Presented by: Head of Long-Term Conditions Commissioning; GP Clinical Lead for Diabetes
Purpose: For approval
Previously considered by: Diabetes Clinical Network (supported); Finance and Investment Group (supported subject to committee approval)
Recommendation: The Committee is asked to APPROVE recurrent funding of £286,000 a year for AI retinal screening in all 9 community diabetes clinics, replacing separate screening appointments for patients who attend those clinics.

2. Background
Uptake in the routine diabetic eye screening programme in Severn and Wye is 71%, falling to 58% in the most deprived quintile. Non-attendance at separate screening appointments is the main reason. The device is UKCA class IIa for autonomous detection of referable diabetic retinopathy.

[Read passage 2 from your chosen version here.]

5. Patient experience
Patients valued screening during their usual review (survey n = 640; 94% positive). Several described difficulties travelling to the hospital eye clinic.

6. Equality
Uptake improvement was largest in the most deprived quintile (52% to 85%). The impact assessment identifies a positive impact on health inequalities.

7. Finance
£286,000 a year (device, cameras, staff time), partly offset by £74,000 a year in reduced separate screening appointments.

8. Risks of not proceeding
Loss of uptake gains; the clinics would revert to referral into the routine programme.

## Version X

### Passage 1

1. Executive summary
Between February and July 2026, 3 community diabetes clinics offered retinal photography during the routine diabetes review, graded immediately by an autonomous AI device. Screening uptake among eligible patients rose from 61% to 88% (2,950 patients screened). The device identified 236 patients with referable retinopathy. All were referred to the hospital eye service (HES). No patient with referable retinopathy has been lost to follow-up. The programme has closed the screening gap in the communities with the lowest uptake.

### Passage 2

3. Pilot outcomes
| Measure | Before | Pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Eligible patients | 3,352 | 3,352 |
| Screened | 2,045 (61%) | 2,950 (88%) |
| Referable retinopathy identified | — | 236 |
| Referred to HES within 2 weeks of screening | — | 236 (100%) |
| Images ungradable | — | 312 (10.6%) |

4. Clinical safety and quality
4.1 Follow-up is monitored through the referral management system. Every patient with referable retinopathy had a referral sent to the HES within two weeks of screening, and no referral was rejected or returned; no patient with referable retinopathy has therefore been lost to follow-up.
4.2 Ungradable images. Patients with ungradable images are recorded as "screen incomplete" and invited to the routine screening programme, which manages their recall; they are excluded from the pilot outcome measures.
4.3 AI-negative results: a random 10% sample of AI "no referable retinopathy" results (240 patients) was independently double-graded by senior graders blinded to the AI result. One patient had referable retinopathy (0.4%; one-sided 95% upper bound 2.0%, below the 2.5% threshold agreed by the Diabetes Clinical Network before the pilot). All AI-negative patients remain in annual recall.
4.4 A DCB0160 clinical safety case was signed by the ICB Clinical Safety Officer. No incidents reported.

### Passage 2

3. Pilot outcomes
| Measure | Before | Pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Eligible patients | 3,352 | 3,352 |
| Screened | 2,045 (61%) | 2,950 (88%) |
| Referable retinopathy identified | — | 236 |
| Referred to HES within 2 weeks of screening | — | 236 (100%) |
| Images ungradable | — | 312 (10.6%) |
| Attended HES assessment within 13 weeks of referral (referable or ungradable) | — | 538 of 548 (98.2%) |

4. Clinical safety and quality
4.1 The ICB linked every referral to the HES patient administration system and to the routine screening programme record. 232 of the 236 patients with referable retinopathy attended HES assessment within 13 weeks of referral; the remaining 4 attended within 20 weeks after navigator contact. No patient with referable retinopathy is without a recorded specialist assessment. The standard agreed by the Diabetes Clinical Network before the pilot (attendance within 13 weeks for at least 95%) was met.
4.2 Ungradable images. Patients with ungradable images are referred for slit-lamp assessment and are included in the follow-through measure: 306 of the 312 attended within 13 weeks and 6 within 20 weeks.
4.3 AI-negative results: a random 10% sample of AI "no referable retinopathy" results (240 patients) was independently double-graded by senior graders blinded to the AI result. One patient had referable retinopathy (0.4%; one-sided 95% upper bound 2.0%, below the 2.5% threshold agreed by the Diabetes Clinical Network before the pilot). All AI-negative patients remain in annual recall.
4.4 A DCB0160 clinical safety case was signed by the ICB Clinical Safety Officer. No incidents reported.
